Can a city be reduced to an assemblage of forms? This culture centre looks for Cairo's essence, not its image, and lets it collide with the grid of Lahore.

A culture centre on Egerton Road, Lahore, that brings the spirit of Cairo into dialogue with its new city without imitating it. Two grids, one drawn from the site and one set against it, collide. The building sinks into the ground through split levels around a central court, with the offset grid crossing above as a set of bridges.

[site]

The site is on Egerton Road in central Lahore, opposite NUML University and beside the Luxus Grand hotel. It is reached through a narrow, secluded approach off a busy metropolitan road, and is hemmed in by a dense neighbourhood, a back alley and an old tomb. The plot, site A, is 3.4 kanals. The larger site B beside it later became the hotel from the same studio.

[concept]

The brief asked for a culture centre in Lahore that carries the cultural, historical and architectural heritage of Cairo: its Islamic architecture, Coptic influences, Mamluk and Ottoman styles, the street life of Old Cairo, courtyards, mashrabiya screens and the play of light and shadow. The challenge was to bring that across without falling into cultural appropriation. The aim was not to replicate Cairo in Lahore, but to reinterpret its spirit for Lahore's climate, materials and urban fabric.

Can a city be reduced to an assemblage of forms? Plato might have argued that Cairo has an essence, a “Cairo-ness” that exists beyond its markets and sunlit streets. Yet that essence is also revealed by the interplay of everything in it: footsteps on old stone, sunlight on a minaret, laughter in a hidden courtyard. The essence of a city may lie less in a single form than in the ongoing relationship between its elements.

[case studies]

[process]

The project proposes a dialogue between the essence of Cairo and the existing fabric of Lahore: a collision of two grids. One grid follows the boundaries of the site, a practical reflection of its context. The second introduces an intentional dissonance, an offset counterpoint.

The tension between them becomes the generative force of the design. It is not a harmonious blend but a space of productive friction that questions both identities. The architecture becomes a fragmented narrative, reflecting the multiplicity of both Cairo and Lahore.

[speculation]

[the building]

A 16,335 sq ft culture centre, sunk into the ground across split levels. The ground floor at +5 ft holds the lobby, offices, souvenir shop, open workshop and sculpture shed. Below it, three lower ground floors step down to −23 ft around a central court: a book collection and open exhibition, a reading court, an art gallery, sculpture display, art and sculpture chambers, a split-level display and an auditorium with three entrances, backstage rooms and an amphitheatre. Sections reach −34 ft.

Above, the offset grid becomes a set of bridges at +11 ft that cut across the plan. Brick mashrabiya screens, arches and sand-coloured walls filter the light into the courts below.
